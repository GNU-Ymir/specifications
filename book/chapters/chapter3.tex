\chapter{Native scalar types}
\label{chap:scalars}

This chapter specifies the native scalar types: their literals, their
properties, and every casting, unary and binary operation the language allows on
them. Compound types follow in Chapter~\ref{chap:compound}.

A scalar type holds exactly one value, and borrows nothing from any other. It
follows that a scalar cannot be mutable in its own right -- there is nothing
inside it to change independently. Mutability reaches it from outside instead,
through a mutable variable holding it, or through a mutable compound type
containing it.
